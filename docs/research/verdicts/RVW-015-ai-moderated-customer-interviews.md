# RVW-015 — let an AI *run* your customer interviews (it "never seeks validation")

## The claim
- **Source:** koji.so/blog/mom-test-customer-interviews-2026 + getperspective.ai (both 2026)
- **Core assertion:** AI-moderated interviews are *structurally better* than humans at The Mom Test — no
  emotional stake, never seeks validation, consistent probing — so let the AI run the interview.
- **Inbox file:** `docs/research/inbox/ai-moderated-customer-interviews.md`

## Rubric
| # | Question | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contradicts a PRINCIPLE? | **Yes — #6 (humane before viable) + the founding thesis.** Removing the founder from the customer conversation is the exact self-deception BOSS exists to prevent ("build without fooling themselves"). Fitzpatrick/Torres: the founder must *hear* the customer; the learning is in the founder's ears, not a transcript. |
| 2 | Evidence grade | Vendor marketing, n=0 independent — both sources sell AI-interview tools. "Structurally better than humans" is self-serving. |
| 3 | Duplicate or sharpen? | Cuts *against* `/canvas` + `/pretotype` + the whole discovery ethos. |
| 4 | Serves / harms? | Harms every cohort — especially `first-product`, who most needs to build the muscle of talking to users. The humane lens vetoes. |
| 5 | Cost / ceremony | Adds a tempting shortcut that erodes the core discipline. |

## Verdict: REJECT
Fails Principle #6 outright — no further debate needed (the rubric's rule). The founder hearing real
customers *is* the validation; outsourcing it to a synthetic interviewer is the pseudo-app trap in a new
costume. Recorded so the tempting "AI can scale your discovery" pitch doesn't resurface and re-cost this
debate.

## If REJECT / NOT-YET
- **Why not:** contradicts the humane lens + the founding thesis (founder-must-hear-the-customer).
- **Non-harmful adjacent (not this claim):** an AI helping a founder *draft* Mom-Test-compliant questions
  *before* they go talk to a real person is fine — that keeps the founder in the conversation. That's a
  different, much narrower claim; if it surfaces on its own, vet it separately.
