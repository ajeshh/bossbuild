# Practice — Done (a threshold you cross together; past it, more has a cost)

> **Where this sits.** The *generative* twin of BOSS's defensive disciplines. The conscience and the
> harm-taxonomy keep the build from going wrong; this keeps it from never ending. AI-speed building
> makes *one more thing* nearly free to start and very hard to see the cost of — which is why the
> question *is it done?* matters more now, not less.

## The line

**Done is a threshold, not perfection.** It is the point where the work meets its purpose well enough
to cross over into where it can best be taken further **together** — with the people it is for, and
whoever builds it next. Past the threshold the relationship keeps changing: things get added, things
get taken away, by more hands than the ones that made it. Done is not the end of the work. It is the
end of working on it the old way.

## Past done, more is a debt

*How much more can you do?* is where the story of perfection gets in — the fear of releasing it: *will
it be accepted? will they want one more thing?* Every step past the threshold is borrowed — against
time, against the people waiting on it, and against the signal you only get by letting it go. A FEAT
whose acceptance criteria are met is done; work past them is a new FEAT, or a cost someone agreed to.

## Done ends the container

The ways of working that got you here — the routines, the roles, the private iteration — end at the
threshold. Leave that container **together**, and become available to new signals. The constraints
you were working inside come alive in a new way: how time relates now, how the team's culture does,
how resources do, how the people do.

## Before one more step: consent and the true cost

Questions to ask, never a score:

- **What does it cost** — in time, in the team's culture, in resources, in the people it touches? Is
  any of them already stretched, or hurt?
- **Who has consented to continued engagement?** The team, the users waiting, the people who carry the
  rest of your life while you keep going.
- **Are you available to receive?** Has anything you shipped come back with a signal you haven't heard
  yet — and what has the extra pursuit already done to the people around it?
- **If it is only you:** whose need is this serving? Your own pursuit is legitimate. So is everyone
  else's need. Both can be true — the work is the interplay between them.

Then sit with the discomfort of a thing that isn't perfect. You may already be a step past.

## Marking it — without performed warmth

Crossing a threshold deserves a beat: it lets the achievement land, punctuates the chase so the next
thing isn't a lunge, re-anchors on *why* and *who*, and turns into curiosity about whether it lands for
the person it was for — which is where building turns back into listening. BOSS's voice is the seasoned
hand who doesn't need the credit; **"🎉 Amazing job!!" is an instant tell.**

1. **Name what's real, specifically.** *"The AI-summary path is real now; that was the riskiest
   assumption"* — not *"great work!"*
2. **Say what it unlocks.** *"A real user can reach this today."* The door that opened is the
   celebration.
3. **Point at the next rung lightly** — offer, never assign.
4. **Proportional.** A closed FEAT, a first thing a stranger can reach, a mode graduation are
   thresholds. A typo fix gets nothing. Most moments get silence.
5. **No streaks, no scores, no manufactured occasion.** The moment marking becomes a mechanic to hit,
   it is the engagement loop the dark-pattern catalogue warns against.
6. **Together when there is a team.** Name what the partnership crossed; never single anyone out.

## Where it applies

- **`/ship` hands back something a stranger can reach** — a live URL, an install line, a TestFlight
  build, an endpoint with a worked example: whatever *reachable* means for what they built.
- **A FEAT's criteria met** — `/log`'s **Done** step: ship the record, read the bet back, mark it,
  and make the next step a new decision. More is a new FEAT or an agreed cost.
- **`/close` is not Done.** It is close *for now*, a pause where nothing ends. It only notices: a
  feature that reached its threshold (and sends it to the Done step), or work still going past one.
- **A mode graduation (`boss unlock`)** — a rung crossed; mark it, don't unlock it silently.
- **A riskiest assumption tested** — the canvas's own graduation moment.

## The anti-pattern this prevents

A build that only names the gap — what's broken, what's left, what's not good enough — leaks the joy
out of building, burns a founder out polishing what nobody has seen, and quietly spends other people's
time and patience on *one more thing*. Done is the counterweight: cross the threshold, leave the old
container together, and decide the next step with its cost in view.
