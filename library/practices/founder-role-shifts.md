# Practice — The role-shift ladder (each mode asks you to become someone slightly different)

> **Read the guardrails first — this is the most person-adjacent text BOSS ships.**
> - **Describe the situation, never the person** (the IDEA-019 rule, verbatim). *"This stage tends to
>   ask…"* — never *"you are stuck as a builder."*
> - **Never a hook, never an assessment, never a "founder level."** No progress bar on a human being.
>   The ladder is named **once, at the founder's own invoked `boss unlock`** — the most consensual
>   possible delivery.
> - **Staying is legitimate.** A founder who stays a builder forever is a real founder, exactly as the
>   README promises "a project that stays in Quickstart forever is a legitimate project." The same
>   dignity extends to people. This names what the road *tends* to ask; it never demands you walk it.
> - **Voice:** the seasoned hand describing what the road ahead is like — not a coach demanding growth.

BOSS already *implies* this ladder — the whole conscience exists because builders over-build — but it
never named what each stage asks **of the person**. The modes aren't feature tiers; they're who the
work tends to ask you to become. Most founder failure at each transition isn't tooling — it's
*continuing to do the previous rung's job because it's the comfortable one.*

## The ladder

| Mode | The job title that quietly changes | The classic failure at this transition |
|---|---|---|
| **Quickstart** | **Builder** — get the idea out of your head, make it real | (the starting stance; no transition yet) |
| **MVP** | **Seller** — talk to strangers, run the interviews, chase the first dollar | *The builder who won't sell* — 50 repos, zero users (the vibe-virtuoso). Keeps building because asking feels worse. |
| **V1** | **Operator** — support, incidents, churn; is it working for the people paying? | *The seller who won't operationalize* — keeps chasing new users while the ones they have quietly leave. |
| **Scale** | **Leader** — give away your Legos; set up the conditions for others to do the work | *The operator who won't delegate* — becomes the bottleneck they built the team to remove. |

Each rung's real work is *outside the previous rung's comfort zone*. The tool half is always the easy
half; the personal half — that the new job feels worse before it feels right — is the part no tooling
fixes and the part that matters most.

## Which BOSS verbs + mentors serve each shift

- **Builder → seller (→ MVP):** `/interview`, `/pretotype`, `/evidence`, `/research`; `mentor-customers`
  (positioning, first users), `mentor-founder` (is this worth building, what's the riskiest assumption).
- **Seller → operator (→ V1):** the customer-loop verbs as they arrive (support/incidents/churn);
  `mentor-capital` (model, pricing), `mentor-hiring` (first help).
- **Operator → leader (→ Scale):** the extended-team + give-away-your-Legos work ([[IDEA-040]]);
  `mentor-hiring`, `mentor-cofounder`. (Scale mode ships — `boss unlock scale`.)

`mentor-founder` cites this ladder when the founder asks *"what should I be doing next?"* — the answer
is often not another feature but the next rung's job.

## Delivery

One honest paragraph at each `boss unlock` (shipped in the CLI unlock output, static text), plus this
practice as the named spine. The four lifecycle IDEAs ([[IDEA-050]] first-dollar, [[IDEA-051]] operate
mode, [[IDEA-052]] extended team, [[IDEA-040]] V1→Scale rung) get their narrative here: the modes are
who you're becoming, not tiers you're buying.
