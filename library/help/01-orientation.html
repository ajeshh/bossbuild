BOSS puts the structure of a company around your code — how to decide what to build, how to find out whether anyone wants it, when to ship, and when the discipline should get out of your way. This page is the whole of it, generated from your project, so everything below is something you actually have.

Two kinds of thing run here, in two different places. Commands starting with boss run in your terminal — they read and write files, and they never call a model. Commands starting with / run inside Claude Code, because they need judgment.

When to use a BOSS verb, and when to just talk to Claude

This is the distinction worth internalising, because getting it wrong is how a tool like this becomes ceremony:

  • “How do I build this?” — just ask Claude. No skill, no verb, no ceremony. That is most of your day, and BOSS is designed to stay out of it.
  • “Should I build this? Is it working? What did I decide, and why?” — that is a BOSS verb. Those are the seams between building, and the seams are where things get lost.

While you are heads-down, BOSS does almost nothing. That is the design, not a gap.

Nothing here is permanent

Every mode is additive and every unlock is your call. Nothing is ever removed from your project by climbing, and the whole installation comes back out with boss remove — which previews first and needs --apply to actually do anything.
