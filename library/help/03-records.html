Twelve ideas, three half-finished specs, and a decision you cannot remember making. BOSS gives every one of them a file, a type and a status — and then renders your progress from those files.

There is deliberately no status document anywhere. A hand-maintained board and the frontmatter it copies will eventually disagree, and nothing tells you which one is lying. So status lives in exactly one place — the record's own frontmatter — and boss board is a read of the files, never a document you keep up to date.

The consequence, which is the useful part

You cannot make the board look better than the work. Moving a card means changing a record; changing a record means the work actually moved. An empty column stays visibly empty, because “two ideas captured and nothing tested” is the honest picture of where you are.

The status vocabulary is closed

A fixed, small list of words — not free text. That is what makes the board renderable and what lets boss records catch a typo before it becomes an invisible card. If you find yourself wanting a new status, the work has usually outgrown its record type rather than its vocabulary.

Evidence is graded, not collected

Signals about whether anyone wants this go on a fixed three-rung ladder — stated-pain → observed-behavior → commitment — and the grade is what somebody did, not how enthusiastic they sounded. “Great idea, I'd definitely use that” is rung one and stays there. Desk research never becomes evidence at all: reading about a market is context, and treating it as a signal is how a founder talks themselves into a year of work.
