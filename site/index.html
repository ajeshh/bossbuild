B.O.S.S.

Build Out Solid Stuff

A startup incubator that runs inside Claude Code.

Everyone can build now. Almost no one can tell a real business from a convincing demo. BOSS puts the structure of a startup around your code — how to decide what to build, when to ship, and when the discipline should get out of your way — and it speaks up when you're drifting. Free, MIT, zero dependencies, runs entirely on your machine.

$npx oyeboss new my-app

Needs Node 18+ and Claude Code. The CLI sets the project up; the skills run inside Claude.

▸the problem

Building got cheap. Being wrong didn't.

AI made a polished prototype nearly free, which created a new trap: confusing a pseudo app — an impressive demo with no proven pain, no workflow fit, no willingness to pay — with a real business value app.

You've been using Claude Code for a few months. You shipped one project this year. There are two more in ~/projects/ you didn't finish, and a folder of ideas you never started. Each time you rebuild the same scaffolding from scratch, and somewhere around screen three the design drifts into 47 different blues — or you realize you never pressure-tested whether the bet was real at all.

Cheap AI lowers the cost of building, not the cost of being wrong. So the cheaper building gets, the more the loop matters.

BOSS helps you build faster without fooling yourself.

Not a guarantee you'll win — a way to decide faster, including quitting faster. That's the honest claim, and it's the only one on this page.

▸is this you?

Three shapes this actually fits

two people who clean houses Forty regulars, a WhatsApp thread and a paper diary. Sunday nights go on rebuilding the rota by hand and someone still turns up at the wrong address. Neither of them has written code before. → boss new
the spreadsheet twelve people fight over It runs a real part of the business and nobody trusts it. Someone inside the company is going to rebuild it properly this quarter, and they can build now — the hard part is knowing what to leave out. → boss new
something already live, already paying The repo exists and works. An AI feature is going into it, and the real question isn't how to build the feature — it's whether it's a good idea and what it costs per user. → boss adopt

Not for two cases, and it'll say so. A codebase that already has a product team around it — you have the structure; this would be a second one arguing with the first. And a weekend throwaway you'll delete on Monday — git init is enough, and anything more is the ceremony BOSS exists to prevent.

▸the idea

What “scaffolding” means here

It's the construction word, used literally. Scaffolding is the temporary structure that goes up around a building while it's being built. You don't live in it. It holds things steady while the real thing takes shape, it gets added to as the building gets taller, and it comes down when it's no longer holding anything up.

BOSS is that structure, and it goes up one level at a time. A funded company gets it from a process, a board and a team; solo with an AI you get none of it. A one-evening idea gets almost nothing here. A thing with real users gets more. You never pay for ceremony the project hasn't earned, and nothing gets bolted on that you can't take back off.

What it puts around you

a process that fits right now Four modes. The lightest one is five seconds and a file you can read in a minute.
people to ask 6 mentors for the parts code can't teach, plus builders who do the work.
a memory Every idea, decision and signal gets a file with a status. Your progress is rendered from those files, so there's no board to keep up to date and nothing that can quietly disagree with your repo.
someone who'll tell you the truth The conscience. It speaks when you're drifting, then stops. You can pause it.

And when it's holding nothing up, it comes down: boss remove.

▸the name

Yeah. It's called BOSS. We know.

Everyone's had a bad boss — the one who hovered, took the credit, and never once told you the truth. This is the opposite of that one. BOSS stays quiet until you actually need it. It never puts its name on your work: you ship it, your name's on it. And it won't blow smoke up your skirt — its entire job is telling you when you're fooling yourself.

You're the boss. BOSS just has your back. The name's a joke. The conscience isn't.

make it real.
$npx oyeboss new my-app

Source on GitHub · npm · MIT licensed · zero dependencies · no account.

Every byte stays on your machine. The only network call BOSS makes is when you type boss update — a GET to the public npm registry asking for a version number. Nothing is sent about you, your code, or your ideas. No analytics, no telemetry, no phone-home; the check never runs unless you ask for it. It’s ~30 lines in src/update.js and you can read them.

Where this actually is: v0.206.0, built in the open and used daily by its author. Very few other people have used it yet. BOSS is its own first project — it runs its own modes, files its own ideas, and its conscience nudges the person building it. If that sounds like a reason to wait, that’s a fair read.
