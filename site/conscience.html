The conscience

It speaks once, then gets out of the way.

This is the part of BOSS people either love or want to switch off — so it's worth being precise about what it does, what it can't do, and every way you can shut it up.

▸a real moment
you've captured four ideas and tested none 
… you submit a prompt — anything — and it speaks once:
 
▸ That's the fourth thing you've added here, and none of it's
  been tested. Who would you talk to first to find out if any
  of them are real? /canvas is one way to pressure-test it —
  but a 15-minute call with the right person beats it.
 
Then it goes quiet. You decide.

Note what it didn't do: it didn't block the prompt, didn't repeat itself, didn't grade you, and it pointed at something better than its own feature.

▸mechanism

how it works

Detection is mechanical

A lightweight hook watches the state of the work — how many ideas are captured, how many are tested, whether a spec traces to anything the canvas validated. When a named moment's condition is met, it fires. No model is asked “is this person drifting?”

Tact is the model's job

Only once a moment is earned does a model compose the actual words — cohort-aware, so the same moment reads differently for a first-time builder and someone who's shipped three products. The judgment about whether to speak is never the model's.

That split is the whole design. A model deciding when to interrupt you would interrupt you constantly, and it would be unfalsifiable. A hook's condition can be read, argued with, and turned off.

▸what it notices

The moments it can speak on

Each one is a named condition with an entry and an exit, not a mood. A few of them, so the shape is concrete:

you've captured, and never tested Several ideas in, none pressure-tested. It asks who you'd talk to first — and points at a 15-minute call ahead of its own feature.
you shipped, and nothing tests it Entry is deliberately the exit of the focus loop — at least one feature actually shipped. Exit is one file. A low bar on purpose: it asks whether anything guards the thing you just put in front of people, not whether your coverage is good.
you're building what nothing validated A spec tracing back to no evidence and no canvas cell. Surfaced as restraint, once.
evidence landed, and your user never moved Two pieces of real evidence recorded since your persona was last touched. The one moment that watches for something going stale rather than something never made — every other one fires on an absence. It goes quiet the moment you fold the evidence in.
two of you, and no shared decision Real work has happened and the decision log is empty. It asks whether you're deciding together or in parallel — never whose fault that is.

Refusals are stated in the loop rather than left to drift: it won't quietly self-heal an assertion to make a test pass, and it says so. And when you'd rather ask than wait to be asked, /drift-deep is the same question run deliberately across the whole project at once — canvas, specs, devlog and the actual code — am I fooling myself about any of this?

▸control

Every way to make it stop

None of these require a reason, and none of them are grudging.

the off switches 
boss conscience pause --for 8h   # the whole thing, bounded
boss conscience mute drift       # ONE moment; the rest keep speaking
boss conscience resume           # bring it back
boss status --conscience         # what's open, what would close it,
                                  # and every override you've recorded
  • It never blocks. Not once, anywhere. It has no mechanism to stop you doing a thing — only to name a cost before you do it.
  • It honors prior consent. Once you've heard a moment and decided, it's settled. Re-raising is how care curdles into control.
  • Overrides are recorded, not scored. /decide captures what you chose and why. Nothing counts them against you.
▸the honest part

How it could be wrong

The failure mode isn't speaking harshly — it's speaking too often, which turns a conscience into a nag and trains you to ignore it. BOSS treats that as a measurable bug rather than a matter of taste: every firing is logged, so over-speaking shows up in a frequency ledger you can look at instead of a feeling you have to argue about.

The judgment itself is graded by evals that get re-run when the model curve moves. It is still a young instrument, and it has been tuned mostly against one person's projects. If it talks too much for you, that's worth reporting — /feedback — and it's worth muting in the meantime rather than enduring.

The line it holds itself to →

make it real.
$npx oyeboss new my-app

Source on GitHub · npm · MIT licensed · zero dependencies · no account.

Every byte stays on your machine. The only network call BOSS makes is when you type boss update — a GET to the public npm registry asking for a version number. Nothing is sent about you, your code, or your ideas. No analytics, no telemetry, no phone-home; the check never runs unless you ask for it. It’s ~30 lines in src/update.js and you can read them.

Where this actually is: v0.210.0, built in the open and used daily by its author. Very few other people have used it yet. BOSS is its own first project — it runs its own modes, files its own ideas, and its conscience nudges the person building it. If that sounds like a reason to wait, that’s a fair read.
