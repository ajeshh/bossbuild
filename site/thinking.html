Decisions

Every call, and what would prove it wrong.

BOSS asks a founder to write down what they decided and what would disprove it. This is the same discipline applied to BOSS — sixteen records, in the repo, each with a tradeoff named and a falsifier committed to in advance. They were written before anyone was reading them, which is why several are unflattering.

▸the shape

A decision without a falsifier is a preference

Every record carries the same five parts: the context, the decision, the why, a falsifier — what would prove this wrong, and by when — and the consequences. Plus who decided, and how reversible it is.

The falsifier is the part that costs something. It is written at decision time, before the answer is known, with a number and a date attached: n ≥ 3 real projects, revisit 2026-11-18. That makes finding out you were wrong cheap and scheduled rather than expensive and accidental — and it makes the record impossible to quietly reinterpret later.

Supersede, don't edit.

A decision that turns out wrong gets a new record that supersedes it. The original stays exactly as written. A decision log you can revise is a log that agrees with you.

▸the calls

Eight of the sixteen

Each one links to the record. The full set is in docs/decisions/.

BOSS never initiates contact with an absent founder

DEC-016 · refusing an engagement lever

No push, no phone, no "while you were away." It binds even where the notification would be genuinely useful — a red build, a failed deploy — because those are exactly the cases that would be used to reverse it. The re-entry read fires when they come back, never states elapsed time, and never arrives as a summons. Falsifier: a real founder, unprompted, asks to be told something while away, and what they want isn't already served on return.

Desk research is context, not evidence — and never becomes a record

DEC-008 · evidence hygiene

Findings live in the artifact they inform, carrying a source URL and a checked date or marked unverified. The evidence ladder stays about people. No fourth rung, no "research grade" — a ladder that grades two different kinds of thing grades neither. Learning three rivals charge $40 is context; watching a customer switch is evidence. Falsifier: across n ≥ 3 real projects, research consistently has no home and gets orphaned.

Position, never a verdict — then do the work

DEC-003 · where the human decides

BOSS reports where you are, with what it can't see stated explicitly — usage, retention, cost, why you built it. No score, letter, percentage, maturity level or traffic-light, ever. Two or three options, each with what it would change about your week, always including "nothing yet — keep building." And the half that makes it fair: if you say yes, BOSS does the migration. Naming a problem and leaving you with it is a critique. Falsifier: an observed founder runs it and asks for a straight verdict anyway.

The mission is unadvertised rather than enforced

DEC-012 · mechanism over claim

BOSS holds a Silicon Valley half (validated learning, ceremony scaled to evidence, quitting faster) and an older one (craft transmitted rather than reinvented, building to outlast the builder) — and declines to resolve them. The range of what you might be building widens; the methodology does not fork. Deliberately small consequence: one scope line in the canvas, not a rewrite. Falsifier: a commons or co-op project runs the ladder and something other than the money cell breaks.

The roster gets subtracted before it gets renamed

DEC-005 · scope discipline, downward

Two agents merged into one rather than three kept and relabelled. The headcount went down. Falsifier: across n ≥ 3 real projects, founders consistently ask the merged agent for the thing it stopped being.

The catalog is data; the dose is a filter

DEC-007 · data apart from policy

The dark-pattern catalog is a data file. How much of it a founder meets at a given rung is a separate filter over it. Splitting the two keeps the policy inspectable and the data reusable. And the falsifier calls its own bluff: if by 2026-11-18 no founder-facing surface has grown past the dose ceiling and the catalog has gained no row, the split was ceremony.

The eval suite is tests, not strategy — so it goes public

DEC-013 · what should be open, and why

The conscience's judgment evals moved to the public tier, on the reasoning that they are tests rather than positioning. Falsifier, and it is a real risk: if by the revisit date nobody outside this machine has run, cited or corrected a case, the claim that the evals are inspectable was decoration.

Un-defaulting its own preferred framework

DEC-004 · killing a default you like

The canvas became an answer store with interchangeable frames, and the humane frame stopped being the default — it became the floor. The framework the author built and preferred lost its privileged position because it had not earned it. Falsifier: two independent failure modes, either of which reopens it.

▸where this is honest

What the records don't have

Outcomes. Almost none of these falsifiers has come due, because BOSS has very few users. The records show judgment under uncertainty and a pre-commitment to finding out — not a track record of having been right.

They also aren't a complete history. Sixteen decisions were recorded; more were made. The honest reading is that the ones written down are the ones someone thought were hard to reverse at the time.

Built by Ajesh Shah in Oakland, California. About → · LinkedIn · GitHub

Every byte stays on your machine. The only network call BOSS makes is when you type boss update — a GET to the public npm registry asking for a version number. Nothing is sent about you, your code, or your ideas. No analytics, no telemetry, no phone-home; the check never runs unless you ask for it. It’s ~30 lines in src/update.js and you can read them.

Where this actually is: v0.274.0, built in the open and used daily by its author. Very few other people have used it yet. BOSS is its own first project — it runs its own modes, files its own ideas, and its conscience nudges the person building it. If that sounds like a reason to wait, that’s a fair read.
