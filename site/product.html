B.O.S.S.

For product people

Ceremony scaled to evidence.

BOSS is mostly a product tool, so it has to hold product positions rather than stay neutral. These are the ones it actually acts on — each is a mechanism you can find in the code, not a philosophy it recites.

▸the spine

The amount of process is a function of the evidence, not the ambition

Four modes, and you unlock them. That single idea is the product: a one-evening idea gets almost nothing, a thing with real users gets more, and each rung names what earns it before you climb — then declines to stop you, because it's your project.

The consequence people miss is the downward one. A project that stays in Quickstart forever is a legitimate outcome, not a failure to progress. Most ideas should die there, cheaply, and a tool that makes every idea feel like it deserves a spec is a tool that makes bad ideas expensive.

▸evidence

Three rungs, and the grade is what somebody did

stated-pain → observed-behavior → commitment. Fixed, closed, and graded honestly rather than generously — "great idea, I'd definitely use that" lands on rung one and stays there no matter how the conversation felt.

  • Desk research never enters the ladder. Reading that three rivals charge $40 is context and lives with the claim it informs, carrying a source and a date. Watching one customer switch is evidence. A ladder that grades two different kinds of thing grades neither.
  • The only reward for real evidence is quiet. The conscience gets less talkative as commitments land. There is no score that goes up.
  • One signal per file. Evidence is an object with an id, not a feeling you carry between sessions — so it survives the week you forget it.
▸the canvas

An answer store with interchangeable frames, not a framework

You never pick a framework. You answer questions, and the frame is a view you switch: the same answers render as the Humane Product Canvas, a Lean Canvas, a Business Model Canvas, or a one-page brief for someone outside. Switching re-renders what you already said instead of interviewing you again, and a cell you haven't answered shows as unanswered in every frame rather than quietly disappearing to make a view look complete.

Two questions ride along in every frame, and no conventional canvas has them: who this could harm or exclude, and the principles you'll hold when holding them costs you something. Un-defaulting the framework was never meant to make those optional.

The humane frame stopped being the default and became the floor — the author's own preferred framework lost its privileged position because it hadn't earned it. That decision, with its falsifier →

▸scope

A roadmap that only says yes isn't a decision

So the roadmap carries a mandatory no-list. It's a short bet-list with a fixed appetite, produced when you need it and thrown away rather than tended — not a backlog, not a persistent planning surface, and never a Gantt.

Each bet's confidence is the evidence grade behind it, which is the part that stings usefully: a bet resting on one enthusiastic conversation displays as resting on one enthusiastic conversation.

▸endings

Ending things is a product skill, and it has its own verbs

Most tools help you start. BOSS takes the other half seriously, because deciding faster includes quitting faster — that's the honest version of the speed claim.

  • Ending happens at whatever size it actually is — a whole project, one shipped-then-zombie feature, or one captured idea you've decided against.
  • "Deferred" and "dropped" are different words on purpose. Name a condition that would bring it back and it's deferred — a decision with a re-open trigger. Name none and it's dropped, decided on the merits, with the reasoning kept so you don't re-litigate it in four months.
  • Paused work doesn't walk back in. Three questions first — still relevant, still aligned, anything changed — so you never build a zombie feature the world moved past while it sat there.
▸who you get

The product voice arrives before the engineering one

Day one includes a product lead and a venture mentor, and no tester or planner — because on day one the question is should this exist, not is it green. The rest arrive as the project earns them. The full roster → · how the work gets tracked →

make it real.
$npm install -g oyeboss
$brew install ajeshh/boss/oyeboss

Either puts boss on your PATH. Needs Node 18+ and Claude Code.

Source on GitHub · npm · MIT licensed · zero dependencies · no account.

Built by Ajesh Shah in Oakland, California. About → · LinkedIn · GitHub

Every byte stays on your machine. The only network call BOSS makes is when you type boss update — a GET to the public npm registry asking for a version number. Nothing is sent about you, your code, or your ideas. No analytics, no telemetry, no phone-home; the check never runs unless you ask for it. It’s ~30 lines in src/update.js and you can read them.

Where this actually is: v0.275.0, built in the open and used daily by its author. Very few other people have used it yet. BOSS is its own first project — it runs its own modes, files its own ideas, and its conscience nudges the person building it. If that sounds like a reason to wait, that’s a fair read.
