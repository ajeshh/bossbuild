Guide

Using the team, rung by rung.

Each mode is additive — unlocking the next never removes what you had, and boss unlock <mode> is always your call. You don't memorize any of this: boss map lists what your project has and what's one unlock away.

▸the one idea

When to use BOSS, and when to just talk to Claude

BOSS doesn't sit between you and Claude. You build exactly as you do now. The split is simple:

  • “How do I build this?” — just ask Claude. No skill, no ceremony. That's most of your day, and BOSS stays out of it.
  • “Should I build this? Is it working? What did I decide?” — that's a BOSS verb. Those are the seams between building, where things get lost.

While you're heads-down, BOSS does almost nothing. That's the design, not a gap.

▸the walk
  1. Quickstart 3 on the team 16 skills
  2. MVP 8 on the team 42 skills
  3. V1 15 on the team 46 skills
  4. Scale 15 on the team 47 skills

You unlock each stop. Nothing is ever removed — the roster only grows.

Quickstart — get the idea out of your head

Capture freely with /triage. Pressure-test one with /canvas — the Humane Product Canvas, filled a few cells at a time rather than all at once. Record what you actually learn with /evidence, and what you chose with /decide. A project that lives here forever is a legitimate project.

MVP — build the first working version

Adds the spec discipline (/spec, with validated-learning and evals fields), the smoke gate, and the demand test — /pretotype, which builds a fake door before you build the real thing. Design tokens get scaffolded here too, the moment there's real UI, so style never gets locked into code.

V1 — make it shippable

The design system becomes something you can look at: /design-library generates a gallery of your components — every variant, all five states — from your code, plus a reuse index the agent reads before writing a new one. Alongside it, full design review (/design-review, /ux-check, token enforcement), cross-feature sequencing with /board, and the next tier of mentors. This is where a database architect and real designers arrive.

Scale — grow it

Adds /incident. Deliberately thin: most projects should never need this rung.

▸after you ship

The post-launch arc

A second set of verbs whose moment only arrives once something is live — retention, pricing, measurement, health, the first dollar. They install with MVP but stay folded away until you've actually shipped a feature, so a founder with one idea isn't read a menu of nine verbs about pricing.

And a project can end at any rung. /sunset exists because quitting well is a skill, and a tool that only knows how to grow things is lying to you about how this usually goes.

▸with a cofounder

Building with someone else

AI made each of you faster — which, left alone, makes a two-person team drift apart. You each work in your own AI session, ship in parallel, and quietly stop deciding things together. The uncomfortable part: that erosion is invisible from the inside. Teams that have already drifted still rate their teamwork as fine.

boss team add @their-handle lights up a shared place to decide (/decide), a shared craft commons (/practice), and mentor-cofounder — the one mentor that coaches the relationship: dividing work across different skill sets, decision rights, and the hard conversations about roles, pace and equity. It serves the partnership as a unit and never takes a side.

All of it is dormant when you're solo. Keyed to GitHub handles — no accounts, no seats, no server, no hierarchy.

▸worth remembering

Two things

  • boss status is the one command to remember. Come back after a week and it tells you where you are and what's next.
  • The conscience is always overridable. boss conscience pause --for 8h for a sprint, or mute <moment> to turn down one nudge while the rest keep speaking. It never blocks.

make it real.
$npx oyeboss new my-app

Source on GitHub · npm · MIT licensed · zero dependencies · no account.

Every byte stays on your machine. The only network call BOSS makes is when you type boss update — a GET to the public npm registry asking for a version number. Nothing is sent about you, your code, or your ideas. No analytics, no telemetry, no phone-home; the check never runs unless you ask for it.

Where this actually is: v0.180.0, built in the open and used daily by its author. Very few other people have used it yet. BOSS is its own first project — it runs its own modes, files its own ideas, and its conscience nudges the person building it.
