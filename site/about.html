B.O.S.S.

Product leader. Innovation catalyst. Builder of thriving systems.

▸ajesh shah

Ajesh Shah has spent a decade as a product leader in companies ranging from a handful of people to the Fortune 500 — fintech, e-commerce, enterprise retail, education and social impact. The work has mostly been the same shape wherever it happened: walk into something complicated, build trust quickly, and get very different people aligned enough to build the thing that looked impossible. Together, which is the part that makes it hold.

Scaffolding is the method underneath that, and it took a long time to get right — sharpened by teaching product management, running Lean methodology workshops and conferences, designing hackathons, and building large-scale art. Structure that goes up when it helps, holds steady while the real thing takes shape, and comes down afterwards. BOSS is that practice as a tool, which is why its sixth principle is humane before viable and why it gets quieter as real evidence arrives rather than louder.

The Humane Product Canvas came out of the same practice, and is free to take. Ajesh is on LinkedIn and GitHub; the decisions behind BOSS are their own page.

make it real.
$npm install -g oyeboss
$brew install ajeshh/boss/oyeboss

Either puts boss on your PATH. Needs Node 18+ and Claude Code, in the terminal, VS Code, Cursor or JetBrains, on macOS, Linux or Windows; BOSS runs wherever Claude Code does.

The source is on GitHub and the package is on npm. MIT licensed, zero dependencies, no account.

Built by Ajesh Shah in Oakland, California; the why is here. Ajesh is on LinkedIn and GitHub.

Every byte stays on your machine. The only network call BOSS makes is when you type boss update: a GET to the public npm registry asking for a version number. Nothing is sent about you, your code, or your ideas. No analytics, no telemetry, no phone-home; the check never runs unless you ask for it. It’s ~30 lines in src/update.js and you can read them.

Where this actually is: v0.319.0, built in the open and used daily by its author. Very few other people have used it yet. BOSS is its own first project: it runs its own modes, files its own ideas, and its conscience nudges the person building it. If that sounds like a reason to wait, that’s a fair read.
