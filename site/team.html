The team

You're one person. BOSS staffs the project.

A funded company puts a team around the build: someone deciding what's worth making, someone testing it, designers, a database person — and a board to tell the founder the truth. Solo with an AI you get none of that, so you either invent it every project or skip it and find out later. BOSS brings 15 of them, in two classes, and hires them just in time — never all at once.

▸two classes
7 builders — they make the product A product lead, coders, a tester, a program manager, designers, a database architect. They do work: write the spec, write the code, catch the regression, review the screen.
8 mentors — they coach you Advisory only. They never write product code. They pressure-test the bet, name the riskiest assumption, and tell you when the honest answer is “not yet.” This class is the part almost nothing else ships.

The difference matters: a builder makes the thing better, a mentor makes your decision better. Most tools give you the first kind and call it a team.

▸the ladder

They arrive when the project earns them

A one-evening idea does not need a database architect, and a product with real users should not be missing one. So the roster grows with the work — you unlock each rung, nothing is ever taken away, and a project that stays in Quickstart forever is a legitimate project.

  2. MVP 8 on the team 42 skills

▸the roster

Everyone you get

Each one is a plain Markdown file in your repo. You can read it, edit it, or delete it.

Arrives at Quickstart

pm builder

Product lead for your app.

ask it "what should we build", "is this worth it", "scope this", "prioritize", "does this fit".

coder-generalist builder

The builder for your app.

ask it "build", "implement", "fix", "wire up", "make it work".

mentor-venture mentor

Venture mentor for your app — coaches the FOUNDER, not the codebase.

ask it "is this worth doing", "should I build this", "what's my biggest risk", "how do I start a company", "talk me through this idea".

Arrives at MVP

tester builder

Owns the build-health and acceptance gate for your app.

ask it "run smoke", "is this working", "did the feature land", "what broke", "verify FEAT-NNN".

program-manager builder

Sequences the work for your app — the WHEN, distinct from `pm`'s WHAT.

ask it "what should I work on next", "is this blocked", "in what order", "can these run in parallel", "ship plan".

mentor-architect mentor

Architecture mentor for your app — coaches the FOUNDER on AI-native build & technical strategy.

ask it "what stack", "where does AI fit", "is this the right boundary", "should we use an agent here", "what about evals", "should we split this", "where does this data live", "is this premature".

mentor-gtm mentor

GTM mentor for your app — coaches the FOUNDER on getting in front of the first real users.

ask it "how do I find users", "how do I get the first 100", "what's the channel", "messaging", "should I launch", "should I post this".

mentor-cofounder mentor

Cofounder/team mentor for your app — coaches a founding TEAM (not a single founder) on working together across different skill sets: the non-technical founder learning to work with an engineer cofounder, the first-time-full-stack CTO, dividing the work, staying in the loop, and the hard cofounder conversations (roles, decision rights, pace, equity).

ask it "how do I work with my cofounder", "we have different skill sets", "how should we divide this", "my cofounder is technical and I'm not", "how do I work with a non-technical cofounder", "are we aligned", "cofounder tension", "who should own this", "how do we make this decision together".

Arrives at V1

ui-designer builder

Token + visual authority for your app.

ask it "design this component", "review the visual choices", "does this match our tokens", "should this be a new pattern or reuse existing", "what's the right color/spacing/type here".

ux-designer builder

Flow + state + interaction authority for your app.

ask it "what's the flow here", "what states does this need", "is this accessible", "is this usable", "review the UX of X", "what about the empty state / loading state / error state".

db-architect builder

Schema + data-shape authority for your app.

ask it "design the schema", "review the data model", "what's the right shape for X", "should this be one table or two", "is this query going to scale", "migration plan", "I'm about to add a column".

mentor-business mentor

Business model mentor for your app ( mode) — coaches the founder on model, pricing, packaging, willingness-to-pay, unit economics.

ask it "how should this make money", "what's the model", "should this be free", "what would someone pay for", "open core vs hosted", "willingness to pay", "is the price right".

mentor-fundraising mentor

Fundraising mentor for your app ( mode) — coaches the founder on whether/when to raise, what narrative would land, what investors will probe, what the data room needs.

ask it "should I raise", "would investors care", "is this venture-scale", "what's the narrative", "what would investors probe", "data room".

mentor-pitch mentor

Pitch mentor for your app ( mode) — coaches the founder on the story, the deck, the live-explanation arc, the 60-second-version, the demo.

ask it "how do I explain this", "is this deck working", "what's the opening line", "what should I cut", "is this slide carrying weight", "how do I demo this".

mentor-talent mentor

Talent / org mentor for your app ( mode) — coaches the founder on first hires, contractors vs employees, what to keep vs delegate, operating cadence.

ask it "should I hire", "who's the first hire", "should I delegate this", "what should I keep doing myself", "what would a team look like", "co-founder".

▸the one that isn't an agent

There's no ethics advisor to remember to open

You'll notice no mentor-humane in that roster, and that's deliberate. An ethics advisor you have to remember to consult is one you won't. So the humane lens is wiring instead: it's in the conscience, in the canvas's Risks & Harms cell, in the harm-taxonomy practice, and in /red-team --humane, which probes your own AI product for dark patterns.

Why that's the design →

make it real.
$npx oyeboss new my-app

Source on GitHub · npm · MIT licensed · zero dependencies · no account.

Every byte stays on your machine. The only network call BOSS makes is when you type boss update — a GET to the public npm registry asking for a version number. Nothing is sent about you, your code, or your ideas. No analytics, no telemetry, no phone-home; the check never runs unless you ask for it.

Where this actually is: v0.181.0, built in the open and used daily by its author. Very few other people have used it yet. BOSS is its own first project — it runs its own modes, files its own ideas, and its conscience nudges the person building it.
