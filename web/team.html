The team

You're one person. BOSS staffs the project.

A funded company puts a team around the build: someone deciding what's worth making, someone testing it, designers, a database person — and a board to tell the founder the truth. Solo with an AI you get none of that, so you either invent it every project or skip it and find out later. BOSS brings {{COUNT_AGENTS}} of them, in two classes, and hires them just in time — never all at once.

▸two classes
{{COUNT_BUILDERS}} builders — they make the product A product-lead who decides what's worth building, a coder, a designer, a planner who sequences it, a tester. They do work: write the spec, write the code, catch the regression, review the screen.
{{COUNT_MENTORS}} mentors — they coach you Advisory only. They never write product code. They pressure-test the bet, name the riskiest assumption, and tell you when the honest answer is “not yet.” This class is the part almost nothing else ships.

The difference matters: a builder makes the thing better, a mentor makes your decision better. Most tools give you the first kind and call it a team.

▸the ladder

They arrive when the project earns them

A one-evening idea does not need someone sequencing the work, and a product with real users should not be missing a tester. So the roster grows with the work — you unlock each rung, nothing is ever taken away, and a project that stays in Quickstart forever is a legitimate project.

▸saying no

Some of them exist mainly to tell you “not yet”

The roster above gives you names and one-liners. What it can't show you is the part that makes a mentor worth having: a default answer that isn't the flattering one.

mentor-capital — one seat, not three How the thing sustains itself, whether to raise, and how the story lands. Most tools would make that three advisors; a real incubator makes it one partner who gets deeper as you do. So its remit grows by rung rather than new chairs arriving: what you charge at MVP, the raise question at V1, all three at depth by Scale. It defaults to don't raise yet — and it won't draft a pitch deck while that question is still open, because the half of it that would help you raise shouldn't get to answer whether you should.
mentor-hiring — arrives last, and usually says wait First hires, contractors versus employees, what to delegate and what you should keep doing yourself. It shows up at Scale and its opening position is don't hire yet, because “we need a person for this” is the most expensive way to discover you hadn't defined the work.
mentor-architect — leads with the AI question Which decisions are load-bearing and which are fine to defer — starting with what actually needs a model, and what you're about to make non-deterministic for no reason. It holds the data and schema calls too. There's no separate database agent: that judgment moved into this one, and the parts a checker can enforce moved into /spec and a migration guard.

A builder that says “not yet” is being unhelpful. A mentor that never does isn't a mentor.

▸the one that isn't an agent

There's no ethics advisor to remember to open

You'll notice no mentor-humane in that roster, and that's deliberate. An ethics advisor you have to remember to consult is one you won't. So the humane lens is wiring instead: it's in the conscience, in the canvas's Risks & Harms cell, in the harm-taxonomy practice, and in /red-team --humane, which probes your own AI product for dark patterns.
