Build Out Solid Stuff

Everyone can build now. Almost nobody can tell a real thing from a convincing demo.

BOSS is a startup incubator that runs inside Claude Code. It puts the structure of a company around your code — how to decide what to build, how to find out if anyone wants it, when to ship, and when the discipline should get out of your way — and it speaks up when you're drifting. Free, MIT, zero dependencies, runs entirely on your machine.

▸the problem

Building got cheap. Being wrong didn't.

AI made a polished prototype nearly free, which created a new trap: an impressive demo with no proven pain, no workflow fit and nobody who would miss it — sitting in a folder next to two more you didn't finish.

Cheap AI lowers the cost of building, not the cost of being wrong. So the cheaper building gets, the more the loop around it matters — and the loop is the part nobody handed you.

BOSS helps you build faster without fooling yourself.

Not a guarantee you'll win — a way to decide faster, including quitting faster. That's the honest claim, and it's the only one on this page.

▸what you get

Two weeks in, you have four things you didn't have

Not features — files. Each one is a real artifact you can open, hand to someone, or argue with.

  • An idea that shrank to something you can test on Sunday /idea

    You arrive with "build me a scheduling app." You leave with one week, one cleaner, one text sent by hand — the smallest version that would actually prove it. That line is the whole job.

  • A signal from a real person, graded honestly /interview /evidence

    A fixed three-rung ladder — stated-pain → observed-behavior → commitment — and BOSS grades what you bring it, not what you hoped for. Somebody saying "great idea" lands on rung one and stays there. The conscience gets quieter when real commitments exist, which is the only reward on offer.

  • A decision with a cheap way to find out it was wrong /decide

    Context, the call, why — and a falsifier: what would disprove this, and by when. Lighter than an RFC, denser than a commit message, and readable by a cofounder who wasn't in the room.

  • One page you can hand to someone outside /canvas --frame onepager

    The same answers you already gave, laid out for somebody who has four minutes. It refuses to render if you have no evidence on file — because a one-pager backed by nothing is the most convincing wrong thing you can make.

Before any of it, /pretotype builds a fake door — a real page you can put in front of real people — so you can find out whether anyone wants the thing while it still costs an afternoon. That's available on day one, not after you've built something.

More than one of these on the go

Your own three side projects, or a room of other people's twelve. Either way the problem stops being how do I build this and becomes which of these is actually alive, and how would I know without asking.

Every project keeps the same record vocabulary — one closed set of statuses, typed IDs, evidence graded on the same three rungs — and the board is rendered from the files, so there's nothing to keep up to date and nothing that can quietly disagree with the repo. boss recap reads a week back out of records the founder was already writing.

It installs per-project, on the machine, with no account and no server: MIT, zero dependencies, no telemetry of any kind. Nothing to provision, nothing to switch off, nothing that phones anyone. What BOSS writes down →

Not for two cases, and it'll say so. A codebase that already has a product team around it — you have the structure; this would be a second one arguing with the first. And a weekend throwaway you'll delete on Monday — git init is enough, and anything more is the ceremony BOSS exists to prevent.

▸where you are

You should never have to wonder which stop you're at

BOSS grows in four modes, and you unlock them. A one-evening idea gets almost nothing here. A thing with real users gets more. You never pay for ceremony the project hasn't earned, and nothing gets bolted on that you can't take back off.

{{LADDER_LINE}}

And every rung names its own entry price before you climb it — then declines to stop you, because it's your project and BOSS isn't your gatekeeper.

climbing a rung you haven't earned yet 
boss unlock mvp

  MVP mode pays for itself when three things are true:
    ✓ an idea captured you can actually build from  (2 on file)
    · at least one real signal on file — something a person actually said or did
        (nothing in docs/evidence/ yet — /interview)
      and you know roughly what the first working version has to do
  The unmarked ones BOSS cannot check — they are yours to judge, not its.
  Missing one? That's fine — but you'll be carrying ceremony you haven't earned.
  Unlocking anyway (BOSS never blocks); the deviation is yours to own.

Two commands answer "where am I" without you keeping anything up to date. boss status opens with you are here and the ladder, then the one thing to pick up next and what you've actually learned so far. boss recap reads the last week back out of your own records — what landed, what you learned, what you decided, what's in flight — and says plainly when a section is empty rather than padding it. ▸the name

Yeah. It's called BOSS. We know.

Everyone's had a bad boss — the one who hovered, took the credit, and never once told you the truth. This is the opposite of that one. BOSS stays quiet until you actually need it. It never puts its name on your work: you ship it, your name's on it. And it won't blow smoke up your skirt — its entire job is telling you when you're fooling yourself.

You're the boss. BOSS just has your back. The name's a joke. The conscience isn't.

don't take our word for it

The claims on this page are checkable.

BOSS asks you to write down what you decided and what would prove it wrong, so it does the same in public. Its decision records are in the repo — 16 of them, including the ones that make it look bad, because they were written before anyone was reading. So is the conscience's own eval suite, so you can run the judgment it will apply to you.
