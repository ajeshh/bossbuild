{{MARK}}B.O.S.S.

For project management

Progress you can't fake.

Every tracking tool eventually drifts from the work, because the board and the work are two different things someone has to keep in agreement. BOSS's positions here all follow from refusing that split.

▸the rule

Derive progress. Never maintain it.

There is no status document anywhere. Status lives in one place — the record's own frontmatter — and the board is a read of those files. A hand-kept board and the frontmatter it copies will eventually disagree, and nothing tells you which one is lying.

The useful consequence: you cannot make the board look better than the work. Moving a card means changing a record, and changing a record means the work actually moved. An empty column stays visibly empty, because "two ideas captured and nothing tested" is the honest picture of where you are.

▸vocabulary

The status list is closed, and that's the feature

A small fixed set of words, not free text. That's what makes the board renderable at all, and what lets the record checker catch a typo before it becomes an invisible card sitting in no column.

If you find yourself wanting a new status, the work has usually outgrown its record type rather than its vocabulary — which is a different and more useful thing to discover.

▸parked

Parked is decided. It is not queued.

Most backlogs are a graveyard that looks like a plan — things nobody rejected and nobody will do. BOSS splits that pile in two and makes you say which:

  • Deferred — you named a condition that would bring it back. A decision with a trigger, and the trigger is written down.
  • Dropped — decided against on the merits, reasoning kept so you don't re-litigate it in four months.

Neither is a queue, and the board says so where it lists them. Paused work also can't simply walk back in: three questions first — still relevant, still aligned, anything changed — so nobody builds a feature the world moved past while it sat there.

▸decisions

A decision record with a falsifier and a signature

Context, the call, the reasoning, the consequences — plus two fields most decision logs don't carry:

  • A falsifier. What would prove this wrong, and by when, with a number and a date. Written before the answer is known, which is what makes it cost something.
  • Who decided — you, you ratifying the AI's suggestion, or the AI on its own. That distinction gets more useful every year, and nobody remembers it unstated.

And the rule that keeps the log honest: supersede, don't edit. A decision that turns out wrong gets a new record pointing at the old one, which stays exactly as written. A log you can revise is a log that agrees with you.

▸in flight

Work in progress is a number, and BOSS watches it

When too much is open at once it says so and asks which one is actually moving. It won't reorganise your work or rank it for you — naming the pile is usually enough, and a tool that reprioritises on your behalf is a tool you stop trusting.

Coming back is handled the same way: boss status opens with where you are and the one thing to pick up, and boss recap reads the week back out of records you already wrote — landed, learned, decided, in flight.
